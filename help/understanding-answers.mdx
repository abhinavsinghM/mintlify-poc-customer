## "I don't know" is a real answer

If Magnus tells you it couldn't find something, or isn't able to help with a particular request, that's an honest answer — not a malfunction. Magnus is built to tell you when it doesn't have what you're asking for rather than guess and sound confident about it. Take "I couldn't find anything on that" at face value.

## Checking an answer that matters

When Magnus answers from your company's own documents (see [Finding Information](/capabilities/knowledge-search)), you can ask it where the answer came from, and it'll point you to the source. For anything you're going to rely on — a policy detail, a figure, a commitment to someone else — it's always worth asking for the source and glancing at the original.

Results from a search aren't ranked by how *certain* Magnus is, just by how relevant they are — so a plausible-looking match isn't automatically the definitive one. If something seems off or surprising, that's a good moment to ask Magnus to double check or to look at the source yourself.

## Magnus writes its answer directly to you

What you see in the conversation is Magnus's actual answer, not a summary of some other structured result — there's no hidden "real" answer that got translated for you. If part of an answer reads oddly or seems cut off, that's worth flagging (see [Troubleshooting](/help/troubleshooting)) rather than assuming there's more information sitting somewhere else.
