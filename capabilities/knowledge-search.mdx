---
title: "Finding Information"
description: "How Magnus searches your company's own knowledge and shows you where an answer came from"
---

Ask Magnus something and, where it makes sense, it searches your company's own knowledge base — internal documents, handbooks, wikis, and whatever else your organization has made available to it — rather than answering from general knowledge alone.

## What this looks like

- "What's our remote work policy?"
- "Do we have documentation on the new expense process?"
- "What did the engineering handbook say about on-call rotations?"

Magnus searches across the knowledge sources it has access to, reads what comes back, and answers using that material — pulling from whichever documents are actually relevant, not just the first thing it finds.

## Magnus shows its work

When an answer comes from a specific document, Magnus can tell you which one — so you're never just taking its word for it. If you want to double-check something important, ask "where did that come from?" and Magnus will point you to the source.

<Tip>
  If an answer matters — a policy detail, a number you're going to rely on — it's always reasonable to ask Magnus to show its source, or to open the original document yourself.
</Tip>

## When Magnus can't find something

If nothing in the knowledge base actually covers your question, Magnus should tell you that rather than guessing. See [Understanding Answers](/help/understanding-answers) for more on how to read a "I couldn't find that" response.
