---
title: "Connected Tools"
description: "How Magnus can take action in the other apps your organization uses"
---

Beyond answering questions, Magnus can take action in tools your organization has connected it to — for example, sending a message in Slack on your behalf, or updating a record in another system, if those tools have been set up for your organization.

## What "connected" means

Your organization's admins choose which tools Magnus can use and what it's allowed to do with each one — Magnus doesn't get access to a tool, or to a specific action within it, unless that's been explicitly turned on.

For example, if Slack is connected, Magnus might be able to send a message to a channel on your behalf, but only if that specific capability was granted — not just because Slack is connected in general.

## Using your own connection

For some tools, Magnus acts using **your own account's connection** to that tool, not a shared one — so an action you ask Magnus to take shows up as coming from you, and Magnus can't do anything in that tool that you couldn't already do yourself.

If Magnus tries to use a tool you haven't personally connected yet, it'll tell you that instead of the action silently failing — that's your cue to go connect it.

## Confirmations before risky actions

For anything that changes something — sending a message, updating a record — Magnus may check with you first before doing it. See [When Magnus Asks You](/collaboration/when-magnus-asks-you).
