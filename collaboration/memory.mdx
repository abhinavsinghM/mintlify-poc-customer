---
title: "What Magnus Remembers"
description: "The difference between what Magnus remembers in one conversation and what it carries forward"
---

Magnus remembers things at two different levels, and it's worth knowing the difference so you know what to expect.

## Within a conversation

Everything you say earlier in the same conversation is available to Magnus for the rest of it. You can ask a follow-up without repeating context:

> "What's our travel policy for international trips?"
> ...
> "And what about for a trip under a week?"

Magnus understands the second question in light of the first, without you re-explaining that you're asking about the travel policy.

## Across conversations

Some facts are useful enough that Magnus keeps them beyond a single conversation — so a new conversation next week can still know something you told it before, rather than starting from zero every time. This is a smaller, curated set of durable facts — not a transcript of everything you've ever said.

- If you tell Magnus something worth remembering long-term ("I usually work from our Berlin office," "I prefer summaries over long explanations"), it may hold onto that for future conversations.
- You can ask Magnus to forget something if it remembered it and you'd rather it didn't.

<Note>
  What Magnus remembers about you is scoped to you and your organization — it isn't shared across other people's conversations, and other people's conversations don't feed into what Magnus remembers about you.
</Note>

## Why it sometimes doesn't remember something

If you mentioned something in a much earlier conversation and Magnus doesn't seem to know it now, it may simply not have been the kind of fact that gets carried forward — a passing detail in a specific conversation is treated differently from a durable fact about you. Mentioning it again is the simplest fix.
