---
title: "When Magnus Asks You"
description: "Clarifying questions and confirmations before something hard to undo"
---

Magnus asks you things for two different reasons — it's useful to tell them apart.

## A clarifying question

If your request is genuinely ambiguous, Magnus will ask rather than guess:

> "Sure — do you mean vacation days for this calendar year, or your total accrued balance?"

Just answer it like you would if a colleague asked — your reply picks up right where the conversation left off.

## A confirmation before acting

Before Magnus does something that isn't easily undone — sending a message, updating a record, taking an action in a [connected tool](/capabilities/connected-tools) — it may check with you first and pause until you respond:

> "I'm ready to post this update to the #project channel. Want me to go ahead?"

This is deliberate: Magnus is built to check before doing anything consequential rather than assume. If you don't respond, nothing happens — the action stays pending until you confirm or tell it to hold off.

## What Magnus won't do

Magnus is built to actually act on what it says, not promise something and stop. If it tells you it's going to do something, it does it in that same response rather than leaving you waiting on a "coming soon." If a task takes a while, you'll see it actively working rather than being told to check back later.
